Randomization in C++ 

The randomization capabilities in C++ are accessible via the <random> header of the standard library. Within the random library, there are 6 PRNG families available for use (as of C++20):

Type name	Family	Period	State size*	Performance	Quality	Should I use this?
minstd_rand
minstd_rand0	Linear congruential generator	2^31	4 bytes	Bad	Awful	No

mt19937
mt19937_64	Mersenne twister	2^19937	2500 bytes	Decent	Decent	Probably (see next section)

ranlux24
ranlux48	Subtract and carry	10^171	96 bytes	Awful	Good	No

knuth_b	Shuffled linear congruential generator	2^31	1028 bytes	Awful	Bad	No

default_random_engine	Any of above (implementation defined)	Varies	Varies	?	?	No^2

rand()	Linear congruential generator	2^31	4 bytes	Bad	Awful	No^no


The Mersenne Twister (std::mt19937) & Xoshiro/Wyrand (Non-Cryptographic): This is your bread
and butter. In quantitative finance, we do not care if an algorithm is "predictable" to an
attacker. We care about speed and statistical independence. When you run a Monte Carlo 
simulation pricing a complex asset, your program needs to generate hundreds of millions 
of random shocks per second. Non-cryptographic PRNGs like std::mt19937 or Xoshiro are built 
specifically to satisfy advanced empirical distributions at blindingly fast execution speeds.
